iLovePDF and Smallpdf are the two names most people think of first for free online PDF tools. Both are solid, established products. But both also share one structural trait that ToolShoppy was built specifically to avoid: your file has to leave your device and get uploaded to their servers before it can be processed.

Here's a straight, feature-by-feature comparison — including where iLovePDF and Smallpdf genuinely win — so you can pick the right tool for what you're actually doing.

Where ToolShoppy wins

  • Sensitive documents. Aadhaar cards, PAN cards, passports, salary slips, bank statements — anything you'd rather not hand to a third-party server. ToolShoppy processes these entirely on your device.
  • No daily wall. Because there's no server doing the work, there's no task counter to hit. Merge, compress, or convert as many files as you want, back to back.
  • India/Gulf-specific needs. Kerala PSC photo limits, UPSC dimension specs, PAN card photo rules, gold rate tracking in AED/INR — built for exactly what iLovePDF and Smallpdf don't localise for.
  • No account nags. Never asked to sign up, verify an email, or hit a paywall mid-task.

Where iLovePDF or Smallpdf might still be the better pick

  • Very large batch jobs — merging or converting dozens of large files at once benefits from server-side processing power rather than your device's browser.
  • Team/API workflows — if you need an API, shared team accounts, or integration into another product, their paid tiers cover that; ToolShoppy is built for individual, ad-hoc use.
  • Very old or low-powered devices — client-side processing uses your CPU, so an old budget phone may feel slower on large files than a server would.

The core trade-off, honestly

Server-side tools like iLovePDF and Smallpdf can throw more processing power at a job and offer deeper enterprise features. Client-side tools like ToolShoppy trade some of that raw horsepower for a privacy guarantee that's structurally true, not just a policy promise: if the file never leaves your device, there's nothing for a server breach, a subpoena, or a data-retention policy to expose.

For everyday tasks — merging a job application, compressing a photo for a PSC form, converting Word to PDF, checking today's gold rate — that trade-off almost always favours the tool that never touches a server.
