Photo preparation for centre and office workflows

A centre operator or individual can use this tool to resize an existing portrait for a separate form, an office record, or another workflow that explicitly accepts an uploaded image. It crops and scales the image to 200×230 pixels, then compresses the JPEG toward a 100KB maximum. Check the downloaded dimensions and size before using it.

This preset is not an official Aadhaar enrolment specification. ToolShoppy does not connect to UIDAI, perform biometric capture, validate identity, or submit an Aadhaar update. Do not use a resized file as a substitute for the capture required by the official enrolment software.

UIDAI directs photo-update requests to an enrolment or update centre. See UIDAI’s update guidance and our corrected Aadhaar photo guide.

How to resize an existing portrait

  1. Confirm that the receiving form or office workflow accepts a prepared image and requires these dimensions. For another size, use the custom image resizer.
  2. Select a background and open the photo. The original stays on your device.
  3. Choose Generate Photo, inspect the preview for cropping, and check the output size.
  4. Download the JPG. Keep the original in case the receiving system needs different dimensions or a new photograph.

A size limit does not guarantee acceptance. The receiving system may also check the pose, background, image quality or require a live capture.

Frequently asked questions

Can an enrolment centre use this resizer?

Yes, for preparing an existing photo for a separate form or office workflow that accepts such a file. Follow the centre’s authorised process for Aadhaar capture and updates.

Is 200×230px / 100KB an official UIDAI requirement?

No. These are general-purpose image settings. They are not a statement of UIDAI requirements or a guarantee that enrolment software accepts the output.

Can this update the photograph on my Aadhaar online?

No. This tool only creates a resized JPG. Aadhaar photograph updates require the official centre process.
