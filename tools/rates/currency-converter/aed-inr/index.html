Why the AED to INR rate moves

The dirham is pegged to the US dollar at 3.6725 AED per USD, so AED to INR is, in practice, the USD to INR rate divided by 3.6725. When the rupee weakens against the dollar, each dirham buys more rupees, and the other way round. The dirham itself does not float against the dollar, which is why movements in this pair come from the rupee side.

The figure above is the mid-market rate: the midpoint between buying and selling prices on the interbank market. It is the fairest benchmark, but it is not a rate you can usually transact at. Banks and exchange houses buy and sell around it and keep the difference, and many also charge a fixed fee.

How to get the most rupees when sending money from the UAE

  1. Note today’s mid-market rate above.
  2. Ask each provider for the exact rupee amount your recipient will get for a given dirham amount, after all fees. That single number is the only fair comparison.
  3. Subtract the rupees received from AED × mid-market rate. The gap is what the transfer really costs you, rate margin and fee together.
  4. Check how long delivery takes and whether the recipient’s bank adds charges on its side.
  5. Rates change through the day, so compare quotes at the same time rather than across a few hours.

Remittance tips for the Gulf-India corridor

A rate that looks better by a few paise per dirham matters more as the amount grows: on AED 10,000, a difference of 0.10 INR per dirham is about ₹1,000. Fixed fees matter more on small transfers, margin matters more on large ones.

Frequently asked questions

What is the AED to INR rate today?

The live mid-market rate is shown at the top of this page together with the time it was last updated.

Why is the rate I get at an exchange house lower?

Exchange houses and banks quote their own buy and sell rates around the mid-market rate and may also charge a fee. The gap between the two is their margin.

Why does AED to INR follow USD to INR?

Because the dirham is pegged to the US dollar at 3.6725 AED per USD. The dirham does not move against the dollar, so the rupee’s moves against the dollar carry straight through.
